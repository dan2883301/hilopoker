How Hesitation is played
  • One card down to everybody, then a betting round.
  • Pass round. The first player gets a card face up. Keep it, or pay $1 and pass it to your left. It keeps moving until somebody keeps it; the next fresh card goes to the player after the keeper. A card nobody wants reaches the dealer and dies.
  • Once everyone has had a turn, anybody who passed is dealt a face-up card they have to keep. Then a betting round.
  • Four pass rounds in all, until everyone holds one down and four up.
  • The trade. Discard one card and take a replacement of the same kind — down for down, up for up. Then one more betting round.
  • The declare. Everyone puts a hand down face down, in their own time. Once all are down, they turn up together: high, low, or both. Highs play against highs, lows against lows, and the pot splits. Declare both and you must win both, or you forfeit the whole thing to the next-best high and low.
  • Low hands. A-2-3-4-5 is always the best possible low, and it also plays as a straight for high. By default any five cards can be a low: no pair beats one pair, which beats two pair, and so on, and lower cards win within each. If you pick a limit on the main menu (9, 8 or 7 or better), a low needs five cards of different rank, no pairs, all at or under that limit.
  • The joker. Plays as an ace, or as any card that completes a straight or a flush, for high. For low, it can stand in as any rank ace through 8, whichever helps most — never higher, even under "any low qualifies".
  • Highest visible hand opens every betting round after the first.
  • Bust out and you can buy back in for $20 or $40 and keep playing.
How Crazy 8 is played
  • Four cards down to everybody, then a betting round.
  • The flop: three community cards, then a betting round.
  • The turn: one more community card, then a betting round.
  • The river: one more community card (five total), then a final betting round.
  • The twist. No fixed 2-plus-3 split. Use 1, 2, or 3 of your own four cards, plus 4, 3, or 2 community cards to match — whichever five-card combination makes your best hand. There's no discard in this game.
  • Once the river's out, everyone picks their combination — the game suggests the strongest one it can find, and you can change it before locking it in.
  • The declare. Everyone puts a hand down face down, in their own time. Once all are down, they turn up together: high, low, or both. Highs play against highs, lows against lows, and the pot splits. Declare both and you must win both, or you forfeit the whole thing to the next-best high and low.
  • Low hands. A traditional qualifier — five cards of different rank, all 8 or lower, no pairs. If nobody has one, high takes the whole pot.
  • The joker. Plays as an ace, or as any card that completes a straight or a flush, for high. For low, it can stand in as any rank ace through 8, whichever helps most — never higher, even under "any low qualifies".
  • Action starts left of the dealer every round — nothing's visible before showdown, so there's no "best hand acts first" the way Hesitation plays it.
  • Bust out and you can buy back in for $20 or $40 and keep playing.

How 7 Card Stud Hi/Lo is played
  • Two cards down to everybody, then a betting round.
  • One card up — a betting round after every card from here on.
  • Another card up, then a betting round. Repeats twice more, so four cards come up one at a time in all.
  • The seventh and last card comes down, stays hidden, and there's a final betting round before the declare.
  • No discard, no community cards — just your own seven cards, three down and four up.
  • The declare. Everyone puts a hand down face down, in their own time. Once all are down, they turn up together: high, low, or both. Highs play against highs, lows against lows, and the pot splits, using whichever 5 of your 7 cards make your best hand for each. Declare both and you must win both, or you forfeit the whole thing to the next-best high and low.
  • Low hands. By default any five cards can be a low: a hand with no pair beats one pair, which beats two pair, and so on, and lower cards win within each. If you set a limit on the main menu (9, 8 or 7 or better), a low needs five cards of different rank, no pairs, all at or under that limit. If nobody has a low that counts, high takes the whole pot.
  • The joker. Plays as an ace, or as any card that completes a straight or a flush, for high. For low, it can stand in as any rank ace through 8, whichever helps most — never higher, even under "any low qualifies".
  • Whoever's showing the best hand acts first each betting round, same as Hesitation.
  • Bust out and you can buy back in for $20 or $40 and keep playing.
How 7 Card Stud Roll Your Own Hi/Lo is played
  • Two cards down to everybody, then a betting round.
  • The roll. Every new card comes down first, not up — you then choose which of your own down cards to flip face up. Nobody else decides what you show; you do. A betting round follows each roll.
  • This repeats until you're down to 2 hidden cards and 4 exposed ones — four cards rolled up in all.
  • The seventh and last card comes down and stays down, no roll this time, then a final betting round before the declare.
  • No discard, no community cards — just your own seven cards, and you control the timing of what everyone else gets to see of them.
  • The declare. Everyone puts a hand down face down, in their own time. Once all are down, they turn up together: high, low, or both. How Little Chicago is played
  • Dealt and bet exactly like 7 Card Stud Hi/Lo: two cards down, a betting round, then four more cards one at a time face up, each followed by a betting round, then a seventh and final card face down, and one more betting round before showdown.
  • No discard, no community cards, no roll — just your own seven cards, dealt in a fixed order.
  • No declare, and no low hand at all. The pot always splits automatically between two things: the best 5-card high hand (best 5 of your 7), and whoever holds the highest spade among their hole cards specifically — the two down cards you started with, plus the seventh card, which also stays down. Up cards never count toward this, no matter how good they are.
  • The Ace of Spades is the best card in the hole for this game — if you're holding it down, nobody can beat you for that half of the pot.
  • If one player wins both halves, they scoop the whole pot. If nobody has any spade in the hole at all, the high hand takes the entire pot instead.
  • The joker. Plays as an ace, or as any card that completes a straight or a flush, for the high hand. In the hole it counts as the best spade possible for the hole-card side of the pot — the highest spade (above the A♠) in Little Chicago.
  • Whoever's showing the best hand acts first each betting round, same as Hesitation.
  • Bust out and you can buy back in for $20 or $40 and keep playing.

How Polish Poker is played
  • Three cards down to everybody, then a betting round.
  • A fourth card is dealt down. Everybody then flips one of their four cards face up, then a betting round.
  • A fifth card down, flip one more of your down cards up, then a betting round.
  • A sixth card down, flip one more up, then a betting round. Now everybody has six cards: three up and three down.
  • Buy a card. Every player still in may pay $4 into the pot to trade away one of their six cards for a new one. The new card comes the same way as the one you gave up: trade a face-up card and you get a face-up card, trade a face-down card and you get a face-down card. A traded face-up card goes in the dead cards (everybody saw it); a traded face-down card is gone and nobody sees it. You always keep six cards, three up and three down. Nobody has to buy.
  • Then one more betting round, and the showdown.
  • The declare. Your hand is the best five of your six cards. Everyone puts a hand down face down, in their own time. Once all are down, they turn up together: high, low, or both. Highs play against highs, lows against lows, and the pot splits. Declare both and you must win both, or you forfeit the whole thing to the next-best high and low.
  • Low hands. By default any five cards can be a low: a hand with no pair beats one pair, which beats two pair, and so on, and lower cards win within each. If you set a limit on the main menu (9, 8 or 7 or better), a low needs five cards of different rank, no pairs, all at or under that limit. If nobody has a low that counts, high takes the whole pot.
  • The joker. Plays as an ace, or as any card that completes a straight or a flush, for high. For low, it can stand in as any rank ace through 8, whichever helps most — never higher, even under "any low qualifies".
  • Each round after the first, the player showing the best high hand opens the betting.
  • Bust out and you can buy back in for $20 or $40 and keep playing.
How Double Stuff is played
  • Dealt and bet exactly like 7 Card Stud: two cards down, a bet, then four cards up one at a time with a bet after each, and a seventh card down with a final bet.
  • No declare. Everybody is playing high. Your hand is the best five of your seven cards.
  • Two winners. The two best hands share the pot, half each, with any odd chip going to the better hand. If two hands tie for second, they split the second half; if two or more tie for best, they split the whole pot. There is no high/low split.
  • High hands. Normal poker high hands, best five of seven. The joker plays as an ace, or as any card that completes a straight or a flush.
  • Each round after the first, the player showing the best high hand opens the betting, same as 7 Card Stud Hi/Lo.
  • Bust out and you can buy back in for $20 or $40 and keep playing.
How Double Razz is played
  • Dealt and bet exactly like 7 Card Stud: two cards down, a bet, then four cards up one at a time with a bet after each, and a seventh card down with a final bet.
  • No declare. Everybody is playing low. Your hand is the best five of your seven cards.
  • Two winners. The two best hands share the pot, half each, with any odd chip going to the better hand. If two hands tie for second, they split the second half; if two or more tie for best, they split the whole pot. There is no high/low split.
  • Low hands. Ace is low, and there is no qualifier: any five cards can win. A hand with no pair beats one pair, which beats two pair, and so on, and lower cards win within each. Straights and flushes do not hurt a low. The joker stands in as any rank ace through 8, whichever helps most.
  • Each round after the first, the player showing the best high hand opens the betting, same as 7 Card Stud Hi/Lo.
  • Bust out and you can buy back in for $20 or $40 and keep playing.
Oil in the water
  • The opposite of Blood in the Water, switched on the same way (main menu or the checkbox at the top of the table). A banner and a note under the game name show it for the hand.
  • The river gets its normal betting round first. Then, if the river card was a spade or a club, a sixth community card is turned over and there is one more betting round before the selection and declare.
  • A red river doesn't trigger it (that's Blood in the Water's card). Only the river counts, not the flop or turn.
  • The Joker counts as a black card here, so a Joker river deals the sixth card too. Blood and Oil can't both be on: switching one on switches the other off.
  • In Random mode you don't choose either: each hand that lands on a game with a river has a 1-in-8 chance of Blood in the Water and a separate 1-in-8 chance of Oil in the Water (never both).
Blood in the water
  • An optional rule, switched on from the main menu or with the checkbox at the top of the table. When it is on, a red banner shows on the felt at the deal and a red note sits under the game name for the rest of the hand.
  • The river gets its normal betting round first. Then, if the river card was a heart, a diamond, or the Joker, a sixth community card is turned over and there is one more betting round before the selection and declare.
  • A black river (spade or club) changes nothing. A red or Joker card on the flop or turn doesn't trigger it either — only the river counts.
  • All six board cards are available when you build your hand; you still use the same number of your own cards and board cards as the game normally requires.
  • It applies to every game with a flop, turn and river. In Random mode you don't choose it: each hand that lands on a game with a river has a 1-in-8 chance of Blood in the Water (and a separate 1-in-8 chance of Oil in the Water, never both).
